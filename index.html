Competitive analysis 19 November 2026 Grocery and specialty retail, United States

OctaBiz vs Catapult

Catapult is the grocery POS made by ECRS and sold in Florida by JCR Systems. It runs in more than 4,000 stores and is built around the checkout lane. OctaBiz is built to run the whole business, with AI agents that do the work. This report compares the two in 16 areas and says what OctaBiz should build next.

The verdict

Catapult wins at the checkout lane and in grocery-specific selling. It reads US benefit cards (EBT chip cards, WIC, FSA/HSA and health-benefit cards), keeps its built-in card readers working when the internet drops, scans IDs for alcohol, runs its own scales and self-checkout lanes, takes SNAP in its web store, and has 37 years of grocery customers behind it.

OctaBiz wins in the back office. It has full accounting where Catapult has a ledger export, AI that carries out work across the business with approval and an audit log, an open API free to every customer, and a published monthly price. Stock, multi-store, loyalty, reports and how it runs come out even: OctaBiz has no servers and covers more ground; Catapult has more grocery depth.

To take Catapult stores, OctaBiz has to close five lane gaps first: EBT on the card reader, built-in card payments without internet, ID age checks, a live scale at the lane, and WIC for the stores that need it. Fuller-service grocers will then ask for nickel rounding, house charge, scanned coupons and online SNAP.

JCR Systems resells it; ECRS builds it. ECRS is in Boone, North Carolina.

How each one runs

The biggest difference is what sits in the back room of the store.

Catapult's design is strong offline and fast at the lane, but every store carries servers to buy, patch and replace. JCR's own service menu shows that cost: anti-virus, backup, network security and a "VPN concentrator". OctaBiz has nothing in the back room. Its weak point is built-in card payments, gift cards and refunds when the internet drops.

What Catapult has that we don't

Grouped by how much each gap blocks a sale to a US grocer.

Needed before we can sell to most US grocers

A store that takes SNAP or sells alcohol will ask about these in the first demo.

  1. EBT on the card reader

    Today a cashier keys the amount into a separate EBT machine and records it in OctaBiz. Catapult reads the EBT card at its own pin pad, including the new chip cards.

    Move: add SNAP and EBT Cash through a card processor certified for EBT, at the till first and online later.

  2. Card payments without internet

    Catapult stores the card payment and sends it when the line is back. OctaBiz's built-in readers need a connection; offline, the cashier runs the card on a standalone terminal and records it by hand, which means double entry and no automatic match.

    Move: store-and-forward through the card processor, with a per-sale limit the owner sets.

  3. ID check for age-restricted items

    Catapult scans the barcode on a driver's license and checks the format. OctaBiz already has OCR and keeps ID photos on customer records, but the till has no age flag on products and nothing that stops an underage sale.

    Move: read the license barcode with the till scanner, block the sale under 21 for products marked alcohol or tobacco, and log who approved it.

  4. A live scale at the lane

    Catapult reads the weight from the checkout scale and opens the produce menu by itself. OctaBiz asks the cashier to type the weight.

    Move: add serial and USB checkout scanner-scales (for example Datalogic Magellan) to Device Bridge.

  5. WIC

    Catapult appears on state eWIC lists. Each state certifies a POS on its own, which takes months.

    Move: start certification early in the state of the first target stores, or work through a WIC-certified processor.

Where OctaBiz is ahead

Each point was checked in production with the test account or in the code on 18 November 2026.

AI that does the work, not only reports on it

The assistant can read and change records through 386 actions across books, stock, POS, CRM and bookings. By default it shows an approval card before any change; owners can let chosen routine actions, such as categorising bank lines, run without asking. Every AI change is logged with who asked, which model acted and why. The same actions are open to outside AI tools through the MCP server and the REST API. It can even ring up a till sale and send the receipt by WhatsApp or SMS.

  • Catapult's AI sits in Cognition, a separate cloud product that about 600 of its 4,000+ stores use.
  • It forecasts orders and answers questions. We found nothing in ECRS's material where its AI changes data for the user.
Question asked in the test account

Which products are below their low-stock level at this store, and what would a purchase order to restock them look like? Do not create anything, just show me.

OctaBiz assistant · 3 steps · 21 seconds · live stock data

Three products are below their stock level, all out of stock with nothing on order. It showed them in a table with CSV export, then listed what it still needed before drafting a PO:

  • quantities, because no reorder point is set
  • prices, because no cost is recorded
  • a supplier name

It created nothing, as asked, and offered to prepare a draft for review.

The books are built in

Every sale, refund, gift card and reward point posts to a double-entry ledger. The rewards page shows points owed to customers as a liability in the books. Catapult sends a ledger export to another package and has no payables or receivables.

Nothing to install in the store

No store server, no VPN, no back-room box to patch. Updates go live as they merge: 358 pull requests reached production between 3 and 18 November 2026. Catapult reviewers complain that updates bring bugs that take "weeks or months" to fix.

One system for the whole business

51 apps: CRM, email, SMS and WhatsApp marketing, helpdesk, phone, HR and shifts, projects, online store, bookings, restaurant and hotel. Catapult covers the store; for books, CRM, helpdesk or HR its retailers need other software.

Stock control Catapult lacks

Batches with expiry dates sold oldest first, serial numbers, landed cost on receiving, bills of materials and work orders, consignment stock, goods on approval, and supplier bills read by AI. We found no lot or expiry tracking in Catapult.

Open by default

Public REST API, signed webhooks and an MCP server. Catapult licenses its API per store and builds new endpoints only when a retailer pays for them.

A price a store owner can see

$38 or $104 a month, on the website and in the app. Catapult quotes per deal; one directory lists it from $25,000.

A way out of Catapult already exists

The "Move from Catapult" importer brings departments, vendors, items with store prices and scale and SNAP fields, customers, past sales and rewards balances. It has not yet run on a real Catapult export.

Receipts where customers read them

Print, PDF, link, email, SMS and WhatsApp. We found email receipts in Catapult but no SMS receipts.

What to do next

Our recommendation. Product and pricing decisions stay with the owner.

Win stores at their next hardware refresh

Target independent and natural grocers with 1 to 20 stores when their store server or lanes are due for replacement. Pitch: no servers, books included, AI that does back-office work, a price on the website. This needs the five lane gaps closed.

Make Rainbow Blossom the reference

Finish the move off Catapult, then publish a case study with real numbers once they exist, such as time to close the month or hours spent ordering.

Skip Catapult's expensive ground for now

Self-checkout, fuel, pharmacy and camera AI cost a lot to build and mostly serve bigger stores. Leave them until the core lane gaps are closed.

Suggested build order

  1. EBT on the card reader. SNAP chip-and-tap through a certified processor.
  2. Card payments without internet. Store-and-forward with an owner-set limit.
